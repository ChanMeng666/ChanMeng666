# Cover-letter template — five moves

Copy everything between the rules, fill the `«slots»`, delete the guidance
lines. Total target: **220–320 words**. Longer does not get read; shorter reads
as a form letter.

Draw the two evidence moves from [`EVIDENCE.md`](./EVIDENCE.md), and take them
from **two different themes** — a second latency number after a first latency
number adds nothing, whereas a shipping number followed by a teaching number
covers two different doubts a hiring manager has.

---

Kia ora «first name of a real person, or "Hiring team" if none is findable»,

**Move 1 — the specific observation (1–2 sentences, ~35 words).**
Name one concrete thing about *this* company or role: a product decision, a
public engineering post, a constraint in the JD, a problem the team has said out
loud. Then say what it makes you want to work on. No "I am excited to apply", no
"I was thrilled to see" — the observation itself is the enthusiasm.

> «One sentence about what they do or have decided.» «One sentence about the
> part of it you want to work on, and why that specific part.»

**Move 2 — the primary evidence (2–3 sentences, ~70 words).**
One system, told as a chain, ending in a number that carries its own
denominator or measurement basis. The chain is the point: it shows the layers
you actually covered, without inflating your role. Scope the role word to the
smallest thing you genuinely owned end to end.

> «Evidence line from EVIDENCE.md, theme A.» «One sentence of what the hard part
> was.» «The number, with what it was measured on.»

**Move 3 — the second, different evidence (2 sentences, ~55 words).**
A different theme, chosen for what *this* role is uncertain about. Hiring a
first AI engineer? Use teaching or solo-delivery. Regulated domain? Use privacy
architecture. Distributed team? Use the async and cross-timezone record.

> «Evidence line from EVIDENCE.md, theme B.» «One sentence connecting it to
> something in the JD.»

**Move 4 — the first ninety days (2–3 sentences, ~60 words).**
Turn the CV's past tense into their future tense. Name what you would want to
learn first, what you would expect to ship first, and one thing you would want
to check before committing to an approach. This is the move that separates a
letter from a résumé restatement, and it is the one most letters skip.

> «What I would want to understand in the first weeks.» «What I would expect to
> have shipped by the end of them.» «The assumption I would want to test first.»

**Move 5 — close and logistics (2 sentences, ~35 words).**
Auckland, New Zealand. Say the timezone honestly and say what you are open to.
Do not apologise for geography and do not oversell availability.

> Based in Auckland, New Zealand (NZST, UTC+12) — «remote / hybrid / open to
> relocation as applicable», comfortable with «NZ / AU / SEA / North America»
> overlap. Available from «date». Happy to talk any time that suits:
> cal.com/chan-meng/30min.

Ngā mihi,
Chan Meng
chanmeng.career@gmail.com · chanmeng.org · github.com/ChanMeng666

## Before you send

- [ ] Every factual sentence traces to `data/profile/` (via `EVIDENCE.md`).
- [ ] Both evidence moves come from **different** themes.
- [ ] Every number in the letter carries its denominator, window, or measurement
      basis in the same sentence.
- [ ] No superlative without an enumerable comparison set ("first", "leading",
      "best"). If you cannot list what it was compared against, cut it.
- [ ] No claim that a *team's* or *company's* result was yours.
- [ ] The six red lines at the top of `EVIDENCE.md` all hold.
- [ ] None of the banned words: `delve`, `realm`, `intricate`, `showcasing`,
      `pivotal`, `leveraged X to drive Y`, `results-driven`, `passionate`,
      `dynamic professional`, `prompt engineer` as a job title.
      (Same list as [`../README.md`](../README.md) — these trip AI-résumé
      detectors.)
- [ ] The company name is right, and appears at least once outside the greeting.
- [ ] Read it out loud once. If a sentence needs a second read, cut it.

## Openings that do not work

Not style preferences — each of these tells the reader the letter is generic:

- "I am writing to express my interest in the «role» position at «company»."
- "As a passionate and results-driven engineer with «N» years of experience…"
- "When I saw this role, I knew I had to apply."
- Any first sentence that would survive a find-and-replace of the company name.
